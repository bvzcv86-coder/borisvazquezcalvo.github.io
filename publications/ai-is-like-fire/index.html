‘AI is like fire’: a discourse analysis of language teachers’ attitudes towards artificial intelligence

A discourse-analytic study of how language teachers make sense of artificial intelligence through metaphors, evaluations, pedagogical expectations and concerns about the future of language education.

Overview

This article examines how language teachers talk about artificial intelligence and how their attitudes are shaped through discourse. It focuses on the metaphors, evaluative patterns and pedagogical imaginaries through which teachers make sense of AI as a technology that is both promising and disruptive.

The title metaphor — AI is like fire — captures the ambivalence surrounding artificial intelligence in education: it can be useful, productive and transformative, but it also requires care, ethical awareness, pedagogical judgment and critical literacy.

The article contributes to research on AI and language education by foregrounding teacher perspectives rather than treating AI only as a technical tool or instructional resource.

Why this article matters

As generative AI becomes increasingly present in education, language teachers are asked to interpret, evaluate and integrate technologies whose pedagogical, ethical and professional implications are still emerging.

This article is relevant for researchers and teacher educators interested in AI literacy, teacher cognition, language teacher education, digital literacies and the changing role of language teachers in technology-rich environments.
