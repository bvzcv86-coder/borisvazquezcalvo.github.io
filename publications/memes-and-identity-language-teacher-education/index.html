Memes and identity in language teacher education

A study of how preservice language teachers use memes as digital, humorous and multimodal resources to narrate language learning, teaching experiences and emerging professional identities.

Overview

This article examines memes as forms of digitally mediated discourse in language teacher education. It uses the classroom activity Story by Memes as both a narrative tool for self-presentation and a research method for exploring how preservice language teachers represent language learning, teaching, emotions, personal trajectories and professional identities.

The study analyzes a corpus of 777 memes produced by 84 preservice language teachers from two cohorts. Through discursive and thematic analysis, the article identifies how participants construct identity configurations around language learning experiences, language teaching, motivation, anxieties, personal affinities and career orientations.

The findings show that memes can complement traditional reflective tools in teacher education by making visible affective, humorous, translingual and multimodal dimensions of language teacher identity.

Why this article matters

The article contributes to language teacher education by showing that memes are not merely humorous classroom artifacts, but meaningful multimodal texts through which future teachers narrate and negotiate who they are as language learners and educators.

It also extends research on digital literacies and participatory cultures by demonstrating how everyday digital genres can become pedagogical and analytical resources in formal teacher education settings.
